Powder Toy 3D
=============

Double-click "Powder Toy 3D" to start.

WHAT IT IS

A physics sandbox in three dimensions. Pour water, tip sand, melt ice on hot
steel, pour lava into a lake and watch it flash to steam. All of it is worked
out from the actual equations, and drawn with a ray tracer.

It runs entirely on this computer and never uses the internet.

MOUSE

  Left drag     Use the current tool on whatever is under the pointer
  Right drag    Turn the camera around the box
  Middle drag   Slide the camera sideways
  Wheel         Move closer or further away
  Shift+wheel   Change the brush size
  Ctrl+wheel    Push the brush deeper into the scene, or pull it back

KEYS

  1-9, 0        Pick a material
  Q W E R T     Draw, Erase, Drag, Push, Heat
  Space         Pause or resume
  .             Advance a single frame
  [ ]           Brush smaller or larger
  Backspace     Empty the box
  Tab           Show or hide the settings
  H             Show the controls
  F11           Full screen

Hold Shift with Push to suck material in instead of blowing it out, and with
Heat to chill instead of warm.

MAKING IT BIGGER

Physics -> How much sandbox:

  Room                 how many cells across. This is the one that gives you
                       more space to build in - the solver is a lattice, and
                       space is measured in cells and nothing else.
  How much material    how many specks can exist at once, up to 8.4 million.
                       The figure beside it is what that costs in graphics
                       memory, so you can see what you are asking for.
  Scale                how much real space those cells stand for. It changes
                       how heavy and fast everything looks, not how much room
                       there is.

Room and material are rebuilt when changed, so the sandbox is emptied. Scale is
free and changes while it runs.

DRAWING IT FASTER

Quality -> Draw with:

  Traced rays          light is followed through the material - shadows,
                       refraction, caustics. Slow.
  Specks               every speck drawn once as a little sphere. No shadows,
                       nothing seen through water, and a small fraction of the
                       cost. Good for building something big, or for a machine
                       that struggles.

IF SOMETHING SMALL WILL NOT SHOW UP

Quality -> How little counts as something. The fields are blurred before they
are traced, which spreads a small amount of material thin, so a droplet or the
first dab of a brush can fall below the threshold and be drawn as nothing.
Lower it and small things appear; raise it and surfaces tighten up.

TAKING THE WALLS AWAY

Physics -> The box -> Walls. "Open sides" keeps the floor but lets anything that
reaches an edge pour away. "No box at all" takes the floor too, so everything
falls for ever.

Material that leaves is gone for good - there is no simulation outside the box to
carry it on - which is exactly what pouring something off the edge of a table
looks like, and it frees the speck up to be used again.

IF IT RUNS SLOWLY

It should already have sorted itself out. The sandbox opens deliberately small
and only asks for more once it has watched a few frames go by quickly, so it
settles at whatever this computer can actually keep up with. That is why the
first second or so looks soft before it sharpens up.

If you would rather set it yourself, open the settings (Tab), go to Quality,
and turn off "Match the speed of this computer". The Low preset and the render
resolution slider are then the two dials that matter.

IF IT CLOSES OR GOES BLANK BY ITSELF

Almost always the graphics driver being asked for more than it could manage in
one go; Windows resets it and everything using it stops. The sandbox notices,
starts again with less detail, and says so. If it happens twice in a row it
will stop rather than keep trying, and Windows Update is the next thing to try
- a newer graphics driver usually settles it.

To go back to full detail after that, open the settings (Tab), go to Quality
and pick a preset.

IF IT WILL NOT START AT ALL

It needs a graphics card that can do WebGL 2 - almost anything from the last
ten years, including the graphics built into most laptops. If the window says
it cannot start, your graphics driver may be out of date or blocked by your
organisation. Try Windows Update first.

Failing that, there is a Start menu shortcut called "Powder Toy 3D (without
the graphics card)". That one always works, but it is many times slower - use
the Low quality preset with it.

REMOVING IT

Start menu -> Powder Toy 3D -> Remove Powder Toy 3D. Nothing is left behind;
the sandbox saves no documents.
